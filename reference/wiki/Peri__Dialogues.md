# Peri/Dialogues

CharacterNav
This page lists the dialogue lines for Peri.

| Summer |
| Tara: There are lots of folktales about fairies trying to trick humans. Why is that, Peri? |
Peri: Fairies tricking humans! Those stories are mistold, every one of them. You see, we fairies put great stock in fairness and reciprocity. But humans are deceitful. So don't deceive me, witchling, or I may have to pay you back in kind.
| You've never been in a real apothecary, have you? This isn't some pharmacy run by mortal doctors. Careful what you touch - I work with sensitive ingredients. |
Tara: I will! Promise. By the way, what exactly are you, Peri? A pixie? 

Peri: I am a FAIRY! To call me a - diminutive - is an insult. 

Tara: Sorry! Sorry! I have a lot to learn about Ravenwood Hollow. 

Peri: Now that is a fact. By the way, thank you for the honey you gave us duing the Summer Festival. Honey makes a perfect reagent. 

Tara: You're welcome. It's nice to get to know the fairy it went to! 

Peri: If you ever need potion supplies, come visit me. My ingredients are the finest in Ravenwood Hollow. 

Tara: I will.
| On occasion, I am tempted to reveal my identity to the rest of you humans. But then I remember how downright odd humans are. That League of the Mind, for example. Who would believe in that nonsense? |
Tara: I didn't realize you kept up with the news in Fairhaven. 

Peri: I don't. Westley is our eyes and ears.
| Are the humans staying clear of the coven circle? It isn't safe for them. If you see anyone who isn't a witch pass through the brambles, you must tell me, witchling. |

| Fall |
| Tara: You said a while back that you had something to tell me about the Gloaming? |
Peri: Yes. The Wellspring that you witches balance - it exists in the fae world. The human world is not, itself, magical. But magic bubbles up from elsewhere. That's the Gloaming. At its heart is the Wellspring. 

Tara: But I still have so many questions... 

Peri: You will have to ask them later. I am a busy fairy.
| Tara: If the Wellspring is nearby, does that mean I can visit it? |
Peri: Of course not! The Wellspring is absolute magic. That is why magic courses through every leaf and pebble in the Gloaming. Only very experienced witches, or very old fae, can make it to the center of the Gloaming.
| You have more questions, don't you? |
Tara: Hey! There's a lot of information to digest! 

Peri: True. Go on. 

Tara: If the Gloaming is so dangerous, why do you live near it? 

Peri: Because we fae must. We need at least a little magic in the ground under our feet. Just like humans need at least a little sun on their skin.
| Do you ever feel the Wellspring becoming unbalanced, witchling? |
Tara: Uh, I don't think so? How would I feel that? 

Peri: I suppose fae have sharper senses. In any case, the Wellspring is increasingly unpredictable. Someday soon, you may have to enter the Gloaming.
| Tara: When did you come here, Peri? |
Peri: Long enough to have seen many Wyldes come and go. Your High Priestess has great expectations that you'll follow in your ancestors' footsteps. I suppose we shall see.
| I hear there is a wedding in your village. I suppose it's easy for humans to commit your lives to each other when your lives are so mercifully short. |
Tara: Do the fae ever marry? 

Peri: Hah! To imagine you might truly want to spend eternity with someone! And to marry a mortal is just setting oneself up for heartbreak.
| Tara: Do you ever think about going home, Peri? |
Peri: Where is home? Anyone I might have known back then is long gone. This is the only home that matters now. Your coven must help us protect it.
| I am confused about your why your village is choosing between two barely competent men to lead. Surely Lina or Amira could do a better job? |
Tara: Yes, but they're not running. 

Peri: Well, they should.
| Perhaps you should use your broom to get around if you don't wish to be watched by your neighbors. Just make sure you have enough levitation potion. |
| I have warned Westley to spend less time in your village, now that they are even more suspicious of oursiders. But the human side of him wishes to be around other humans. |
| Zephyr's creations can help you with your farm chores. Then you can spend more time on your magical training. |
| You must take care of your health. You're of no use to the coven if you're laid up in bed. |
Tara: Oh, well I'm fine. Amira fixed me right up. 

Peri: I'm sure she did.
| I'm glad it will be Winter soon. More of the animals will be in hibernation, which means Aryel will spend less energy fretting over them. I'm fond of Aryel but he can be quite exhausting to be around when Spring is in full bloom and there are hatchlings everywhere. |
| I've lived on this island for so long - centuries, in human-time - and I still find new herbs on occasion. It's astounding! |
| You have the sweetest green eyes, witchling. But don't get the wrong idea! I don't dally with humans like that… anymore. |
| You humans have it so easy. You can't live long enough to forget where you came from. |
| Westley tells me someone in your village was accused of being a witch. |
Tara: Yes, but it wasn't someone in our coven. I feel terrible. Sophia's completely innocent. Not that there's anything wrong with being… what we are. 

Peri: You must be more careful. If they ensnare one of you, you won't be able to keep the Wellspring in balance. Then more innocents will suffer.
| You have lost so many who might have helped you in this moment. Gloria, Markus, now Hazel. Now we will see if this coven can still stand against the forces of dark magic. |
| Tara: Before I met you, I thought fairies were… I don't know… glittery? And pink? And sweet? |
Peri: Eugh. Please, Tara, we are not cakes.
| Sometimes, Aryel's antics are just too much. He is five hundred years old! Not five! |

| Winter |
| Tara: How come you never challenge anyone to backgammon? |
Peri: Feh. I have played for so many years that I need something new to keep the game fresh. Like a wager. Money, currings of faraway plants, a kiss… None of which I will ask of you. 

Tara: Awwww.
| The damage to the bramble gate disquiets me. The gate was marked by a witch's magic. But all the witches on the island are part of the coven. Are they not? |
| When Westley first arrived here, he had me attempt many variations on a lycanthropy cure. None of them worked. Now he has stopped asking. Perhaps he has finally accepted the fae part of himself. |
| I overheard Westley and Zephyr discussing their siblings. And how they miss them. Do I have any siblings? And if I did, would I miss them? |
| Violet has grown to look much like her mother. I hope that Gloria will see this resemblance one day… |
Tara: You think we'll find Gloria? 

Peri: I still feel echoes of Gloria's magic where it touched the Wellspring. I am optimistic.
| I am told the ferry no longer works. Why is this a problem? Can you not simply swim to the mainland? |
Tara: That's a long swim. In very, very cold water.  

Peri: Ah. Right. Perhaps try evolving wings? 

Tara: …
| Tara: Hhhhnnngh. It's so cold in Fairhaven today. I got snow in my boots! |
Peri: The snow will stop in a day or two, yes? 

Tara: I know, I know. It feels like a second to you. But no pain feels more eternal than wet socks.
| Will you be providing a table of offerings to us at the Winter Feast? |
Tara: Uh… tone-wise, this might not be the best time to leave public offerings for the fae. You know. In front of Otto. 

Peri: Hmph.
| I don't envy Lina. She has had to carry more burdens than many of her ancestors who were High Priests and Priestesses. |
Tara: And how many of those have you known? 

Peri: Each Dahl who lies sleeping in the Fairhaven cemetery, I have known. 

Tara: That really hurts my brain to think about.
| Damon's parents are extraordinary witches. It's a shame they chose to leave Fairhaven. Your coven could have used their help. |
| So the two barely competent men are at each other's throats, hmm? Perhaps you should send all human men to another island. For safekeeping. |
Tara: Otto and Marty make that sound tempting. But I don't think it would solve the problem.
| One wraith is trouble. Two is a hazard. But three? And in such quick succession? That is an omen. |
| The newest witch asked after my bracelets. He said they were beautiful. I… have never thought of them as beautiful. But perhaps he is right. |
| In all my time here, the coven has always turned the season, despite any obstacles. But the challenge you face… it is extreme. |
Tara: Thanks for the pep talk.  

Peri: What? I am truthful.
| I have heard the news. Otto met with a shadowy woman in the woods? But who? |
Tara: You don't have any ideas? 

Peri: Oh, witchling, I make it my business to know about shadowy women in the woods.
| Upgrading the wand again, are we? I would caution you against venturing too deep into the gloaming, young mini-witch. I once knew a witch — well in truth he was a warlock. The most powerful one I have ever met. He was seduced by the Wellspring's magic. He went mad with his desire for more power. Sometimes I wonder if he became one of those lost wraiths in the Gloaming... |

| Epilogue |
| Centuries ago, humans used to come to me during Spring and request love potions. |
Tara: And did you grant their requests? 

Peri: Of course not. You cannot make someone fall in love with you through potions alone.
| You may have restored balance to the Wellspring, but you must still improve as a witch. And that means acquiring the finest ingredients for your potions. |
| Vanessa's resolve, her single-mindedness, her cleverness… Parts of her remind me of myself. It is startling. I wonder… have I ever sounded like her? |
